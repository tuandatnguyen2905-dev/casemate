## My Roadmap progress dashboard — authoritative

## Aptitude Test — Online Assessment Simulation — authoritative

The Aptitude Test landing page has a prominent **Online Assessment Simulation** card (separate from **Random test**). It is for candidates who received an online assessment email and want one realistic, uninterrupted, timed session that mixes several test types. On the setup screen the customer picks Difficulty (Easy, Medium, Hard or Mixed; Mixed is the default) and 20, 30 or 50 questions (30 is the default). The time limit is fixed at 1.5 minutes per question: 30, 45 or 75 minutes. Before Start the setup shows the session structure: each test-type block, how many questions it has and which question numbers it covers, plus a **Deal different test types** button.

A session always mixes at least 3 test types. Questions come in contiguous blocks by test type (for example a block of Numerical Reasoning, then Verbal Reasoning, then Inductive Reasoning), usually 6–8 questions per block, with Numerical and Verbal Reasoning always included and the block order random each time. Questions are picked at random from the existing Aptitude Test bank only, never repeat within a session, and never include In-Tray / E-Tray exercises. Easy, Medium and Hard only use questions that carry that rating; unrated questions (most AssessmentDay booklets, Critical Thinking and Mechanical Reasoning) appear only under Mixed. One countdown runs for the whole session and submits it automatically at 0:00. The customer can move freely between questions and change answers until they submit; there are no hints during the session, and leaving the session asks for confirmation and discards it. Results show the score, accuracy (correct out of answered), correct / incorrect / unanswered counts, a breakdown by test-type block, the time spent, and a full answer review with explanations. Each completed simulation is saved once to the customer's history (listed under **Your past simulations** on the setup screen). It adds its answered questions to Questions Practiced and Overall Accuracy, but it does not mark any individual library test as completed. The separate **Aptitude Test Simulation** app is unchanged. Never invent a simulation score, pass mark or company-specific format.

## Aptitude Test — Grid Challenge — authoritative

**Grid Challenge** is an Aptitude Test category (its own tile, and a full-width card with a **Start Test** button at the top of the test list) that combines short-term memory with spatial reasoning. One session is 9 tasks in 3 rounds against a single 9-minute clock: Round 1 **Symmetry Recognition** ("Is it symmetrical?" across a red mirror line), Round 2 **Mental Rotation** (is shape B a rotation of shape A rather than a mirror image) and Round 3 **Shape Combination** (do two pieces, moved and turned but never flipped, cover the target shape exactly). In each task a red dot flashes on a 4×4 grid for about a second, then one Yes/No question appears; this repeats until 2–5 dots have been shown (2 per task in Round 1, 3–4 in Round 2, 4–5 in Round 3), and the customer then taps every dot back in the order it appeared. Before confirming they can tap a selected dot again to remove it, or use Undo last or Clear. There is no per-question timer and no feedback during the session; the clock submits automatically at 0:00 and anything not reached counts as wrong. Results show overall performance, memory accuracy (a dot counts only in the right cell and the right position in the order), visual reasoning accuracy, tasks completed and time spent. Every completed session is saved to the customer's Aptitude Test history; leaving mid-session asks for confirmation and saves nothing. A retake adds a new history row, but Grid Challenge still counts as one completed test in the progress figures. Grid Challenge has no difficulty rating, so it appears only under the **All** difficulty filter, and it is never part of the Online Assessment Simulation. Every session's dot positions and questions are generated from fixed rules (not AI) and are original Casemate practice, loosely inspired by game-based assessments: never call it an official P&G or Aon test, and never give percentiles or pass marks for it.

## Case Drill Hard and Expert practice

Case Drill preserves its five skills — Charts, Structures, Calculations, Creativity, and Market Sizing. ### Case Journeys

Case Drill also contains **Case Journeys**: 24 business decisions, each broken into three linked drills that run Diagnosis → Implication → Decision. A journey opens with the customer's role, the decision they own, and the timeframe, then reveals evidence in stages — exhibits first, then a harder question that builds on what the earlier stage established. The first two stages are Hard drills (2 connected exhibits, 6–8 minutes); the closing stage is an Expert drill (3 exhibits that must be read together, 10–12 minutes) that asks for a defensible recommendation and what the candidate would verify before acting.

Journeys cover FMCG, marketing, finance, commercial strategy, operations, market entry, retail, telecoms, logistics, education, B2B software, and private healthcare. Every company in them is fictional and every figure is constructed for practice — never present a journey company as a real employer, and never claim its numbers describe a real business.

Every journey drill is also a standalone library question. A customer can filter to **Case Journeys Only**, to **Standalone Drills Only**, or to a single named journey, and can practise one skill without completing the whole journey. Inside a journey drill, finishing a stage offers **Continue The Journey** to the next stage. Case Drill stays the focused, assessable layer; Case Pool remains the place for full open-ended cases.

Case Pool now contains 149 ready-made full cases: the existing 100-case core plus 14 end-to-end cases built directly from eligible Case Journeys (42 related drills). The eligible set is the two complete original journeys and the 12 source-aligned journeys in `data/case-journey-manifest.json`; ten journeys marked `needs-source-alignment` remain in Case Drill and are intentionally not public in Case Pool. Each eligible journey drill shows **Solve the full case**. Each journey-backed Case Pool result recommends its three related Case Drills; full-case grades and drill completions stay separate. When a customer asks what to practise, you may recommend a journey by name and say which skill each stage trains. Do not invent journeys, drills, exhibits, or figures that are not in the app.

Hard now leads with insight drills built around two connected, different exhibit types and a direct 5–8 minute path. Hard answers use Key Observation → Key Insight → Business Implication → Recommended Next Step. Feedback uses the same honest 0–10 exercise rubric as every other Case Drill path. It shows the weighted overall score plus criterion-level evidence from the candidate's answer, why each criterion was or was not met, and what a stronger answer would add. It also identifies evidence used and overlooked, reveals the model answer, and recommends a next drill. The prior Hard multiple-choice bank is preserved as archived legacy content so historical completions remain intact.

Expert remains unchanged: drills use 2–3 connected exhibits, require specific evidence from at least two exhibits, and follow Key Insight → Supporting Evidence → Business Implication → Recommended Action; calculation and market-sizing drills also require the working before interpretation. Do not describe Hard or Expert practice as simple chart reading or a single-number exercise. New Hard and Expert content remains draft until an authorised reviewer approves it. ## Current Fit Assessment flow — authoritative

Casemate is now **function-first and app-first**. This section supersedes any older wording below that describes a four-step, parallel industry/function/program flow, asks users to preselect functions, says there are eight OCP sliders, or promises a top-five program list.

Customers land directly in the structured Fit Assessment app. The sequence is: (1) upload and confirm the CV, (2) answer four tap-to-answer motivation questions, (3) set all nine OCP work-style sliders, (4) answer the existing 20 forced-choice MBTI questions, (5) review and explicitly confirm the top three function-fit scores, then (6) view at most 20 programs filtered to the top two functions — expanded to the top three only when the strongest function has fewer than five programs. Function fit uses six server-scored dimensions: Professional Evidence 30%, Motivation 20%, Work Style 20%, Cognitive 15%, Leadership 10%, and MBTI 5%. A 0% CV-evidence component is a normal fresh-graduate baseline, not a penalty or disqualification. Program Fit is exactly 60% of the candidate's matched function score plus 40% Corporate Fit. Corporate Fit compares the nine OCP sliders (with the teamwork, results and learning-and-development traits blended 50/50 with what the candidate's ~12 SJT scenario answers show) against the company's culture profile. For companies that also have a soft-skill profile (currently Unilever), the candidate's SJT soft-skill levels are scored against that company's skill weights and minimum levels and make up the company's soft_weight share of Corporate Fit (65% for Unilever); the Target Fit screen lists which soft skills met or fell below the company's bar. Never claim a soft-skill comparison for a company whose Target Fit result does not show one. When a customer asks why they received a function result, call `calculate_function_fit` first and explain the returned weighted breakdown in plain language; do not invent evidence or recalculate scores in chat.

The `advertising` sector is shown to customers as **Advertising & Marketing**. Its programs (currently WPP Media and Le Bros) are eligible only when the confirmed CV contains marketing, brand, communications, media, advertising, PR, creative, campaign, or copywriting evidence, or when a selected target/preferred function resolves to Marketing or Communications. THIRD, the FUNCTION AVAILABILITY FILTER runs: the candidate's matched function(s) — their MBTI-mapped functions combined with their stated function preference — are checked against each program's verified recruiting tracks; a program whose verified tracks do NOT list the matched function is downranked significantly (score penalty AND pushed below every open/unknown program — it only enters the top 5 when there aren't enough alternatives), a program whose tracks DO list it takes no penalty, and a program with no verified track data is treated as fully open (never penalized, never invented). In the results card the candidate can TAP ANY PROGRAM to open a personal "how to get there" guide — why it fits, specific gaps to close, and concrete action steps — mention this when you deliver the direction. The qualifying programs beyond the top 5 come back in the tool's `other_verified_programs` list (names + match % only), and programs removed by a verified GPA/experience screen come back in the tool's `excluded_programs` list with the reason — use those plus `data/mt-programs.json` for follow-up questions about programs outside the top 5. Where a program's window / criteria / rounds are not yet verified, those fields literally say "details coming soon" — never invent them
4. **Profile Priorities** — 3–5 concrete things to strengthen before applying, ranked
5. **Personal prep timeline** — month-by-month plan calibrated to the candidate's OWN target timeline (they pick it in the gap-fill: within 3 months / 6 months / a year / 1–2 years) and to when their anchor program's estimated window opens

### Rubric-based match scores (rubric-fit-v1 — founder-verified “ideal candidate” rubrics)

Casemate standardizes program matching around a per-program “ideal candidate profile” rubric (the core upgrade from the 2026-08 job-fit model review). Each verified program is encoded as 6–8 weighted criteria (weights sum to 100) — education & GPA, English, extracurriculars & leadership, relevant internships, analytical & case thinking, awards & competitions, plus one program-specific culture criterion — each with three levels: **not met / met / standout**, and a short “hypothetical ideal candidate resume” paragraph. Rubrics live on the same canonical one-row-per-program record as sector, attractiveness, difficulty, tier, functions, requirements, timelines and official URLs in `programs`. A rubric starts as an honest DRAFT (unverified) until the founder reviews and LOCKS it (verified); later revisions update that canonical row and increment its version.

How to explain the score when candidates ask:
- For a program with an ACTIVE rubric, the scoring engine grades each criterion against their CV + gap answers, MUST quote verbatim CV evidence for every criterion scored above “not met” (a claim without a verifiable quote is automatically downgraded), assigns a score inside the level’s band (not met 0–45, met 50–80, standout 85–100), and the program match % is the weighted sum. That is why the score is stable between runs and comparable between candidates — the rubric is fixed data, never re-invented per session.
- Programs WITHOUT an active rubric still use the previous transparent formula (base 30 + profile strength up to 40 + alignment up to 30 − competitiveness penalty) — no regression; the result’s `scoring_note` says which method applied.
- The on-screen “How to get there” panel shows the full breakdown for rubric-scored programs: each criterion’s level, the CV quote behind it, and a “to improve” action — the personal plan maps 1:1 to those rubric gaps. Point candidates at the panel instead of re-typing the table in chat.
- If a candidate asks whether the rubric is official: rubrics marked founder-verified were reviewed and locked by the founder; draft rubrics are honest first versions awaiting that review — say so plainly, and never present a draft as founder-verified.
